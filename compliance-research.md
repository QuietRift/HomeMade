## Michigan Cottage Food Law — What to know

Michigan's Cottage Food Law (PA 113 of 2010, amended) allows home-based food businesses to sell certain products **without a food establishment license**, but with significant restrictions.

### What IS allowed under cottage food law
- Baked goods (bread, cookies, cakes, pastries)
- Jams, jellies, preserves
- Candy and confections
- Dried herbs and herb blends
- Roasted nuts
- Cereals, granola, trail mix

### What is NOT allowed under cottage food law
- Meat, poultry, or seafood dishes
- Dairy-based products (unless shelf-stable)
- Most cooked prepared meals (tamales, stews, curries, rice dishes)
- Any product requiring temperature control for safety
- Canned goods with low-acid vegetables

### Key restrictions
- Annual sales cap: **$25,000/year per cottage food operation**
- Must be sold **direct to consumer only** (no wholesale, no retail stores)
- Products must be **labeled** with: producer name/address, ingredients, allergens, net weight, and the statement "Made in a home kitchen that is not inspected by the Michigan Department of Agriculture and Rural Development"
- No delivery allowed under cottage food exemption — **pickup only**

### Bottom line for Homemade
Most of our target chefs (Yemeni, Mexican, Bangladeshi, Southern cooking) sell **prepared cooked meals** that fall outside cottage food law. This is the key risk to resolve.

## Three scenarios — know which one you're in before building

### Scenario A: Home kitchens workable with basic licensing ✅
*Most likely for baked goods / cottage food items*

Chefs get a food handler's license (~$15), follow labeling rules, operate from home. Your onboarding checklist includes licensing steps. Unit economics stay clean — no kitchen overhead eating into the 80% payout.

**Action:** Build a chef onboarding checklist that includes licensing steps and label requirements.

---

### Scenario B: Chefs need commercial kitchen for prepared meals ⚠️
*Most likely scenario for cooked dishes*

Partner with Detroit Kitchen Connect or similar. Negotiate a group rate for Homemade chefs. Chefs pay for kitchen time separately — adjust onboarding to make this frictionless.

**Unit economics adjustment example:**
- $15/hr kitchen rental × 3 hrs cook time = $45 kitchen cost per session
- Chef cooks 15 portions × $14 avg = $210 gross
- Chef keeps 80% = $168 — minus $45 kitchen = **$123 net** (~59% effective take)
- Still meaningfully better than gig work, but brief chefs honestly upfront

**Action:** Call Detroit Kitchen Connect this week. Get rates. Explore group membership for Homemade chefs.

---

### Scenario C: Very restrictive environment 🔴
*Unlikely but possible*

Launch Phase 1 with cottage-food-eligible items only (baked goods, jams, packaged goods). This limits the chef pool but keeps you legally clean. Reframe Phase 1 as a "Detroit home bakery marketplace" and expand categories once you have a commercial kitchen partnership.
